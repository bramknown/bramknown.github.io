Crisis in the Honey Industry

Economic & Operational Pressures

  • Time Consuming – Weekly manual hive inspections
  • Labor Intensive – Labour can consume a large share of operating budgets
  • Disturbance – Opening hives stresses colonies and weakens them

Major Biological Threats

Small Hive Beetles

Invasive pests that destroy comb and contaminate honey.

American Foulbrood

Highly contagious bacterial disease that can wipe out colonies.

Varroa Mite

Parasitic mite that vectors viruses and is a leading cause of colony loss.

Traditional monitoring relies on beekeepers suiting up, opening hives weekly, and checking for signs of trouble. Early detection is often too late, leading to massive kill-offs and economic devastation for farmers who depend on healthy pollination.

3-Minute Pitch Script

Ladies and gentlemen, imagine a world without bees. No honey on your toast and a staggering 30% drop in global food production. Bees aren't just insects — they are an ecosystem, the unsung heroes pollinating one-third of our food supply, contributing billions to the economy.

But today, they're in crisis. The bee industry is facing an existential threat: colony collapse disorder, deadly diseases like American foulbrood and invasive pests such as the Varroa mite are wiping out hives at an alarming rate. Traditional monitoring relies on manual inspections — beekeepers suiting up, opening hives weekly, checking for signs of trouble. It's time-consuming, labour-intensive and disturbs the bees, which can stress them and weaken the colony. Labour costs alone can eat up a large share of a beekeeper's budget, and early detection is often too late. Leading to massive kill-offs and economic devastation for farmers who depend on healthy pollination.

That's where our revolutionary technology comes in. I'm thrilled to present HiveMind, the future of beekeeping. Our solar-powered smart hives integrate laser technology, UV cleaning systems, and advanced sensors to monitor critical hive conditions in real-time — temperature, humidity, sound, vibrations and CO₂ levels. These systems enable us to detect early signs of diseases, Varroa mites, queen health issues, or swarming risks before they become critical. Powered by sophisticated machine learning, HiveMind predicts potential problems with high accuracy, enabling beekeepers to act swiftly. By automating inspections and incorporating honey collection, our system reduces labour cost and minimizes hive disturbances.

Scalable and designed for seamless integration with existing hives, HiveMind empowers beekeepers of all scales to embrace a smarter, more sustainable approach, ensuring healthier hives and a thriving future for beekeeping. With the global beekeeping market projected to hit $20 billion, we're tapping into a hungry demand for tech-driven sustainability. To bring HiveMind from prototype to production, we're seeking seed investment for research and development, testing and manufacturing. This isn't just funding a product — it's investing in food security and a thriving planet. Together, we can empower the agriculture industry to be more productive, resilient and bee-friendly.

Thank you for your time and attention. Let's make hives smarter and our future sweeter.

Bram Spriensma – Use-Inspired Research

Student 1 Material extracted from the 2025 ENGG7902 Mid-Semester Human-Centred Design document.

Questions for End-Users (Beekeepers)

  1. How many bee hives do you have?
  2. How much would you spend on a product to reduce impact and improve monitoring of the beehive?
  3. Is there any current technology that you use to reduce impact?
  4. Are you impacted by pests and diseases?
  5. What is the estimated honey you produce per week per bee hive?

Interview Focus Areas

Interviews conducted by Bram Spriensma explored:

  • User groups: end-users (commercial & hobbyist beekeepers), stakeholders, and analogous inspiration sources.
  • Assumptions before interviews versus reflections after (using 4R reflective writing: Report, Relate, Reason, Reconstruct).
  • Key concerns around labour, disease detection, cost willingness-to-pay, and trust in new technology.

Insight direction: Beekeepers need solutions that are non-invasive, affordable, and deliver clear early-warning value. Trust is built through transparent data, proven accuracy, and minimal disruption to the colony.
